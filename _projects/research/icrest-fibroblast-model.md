## Summary

Scars form when inflammation after a deep wound does not switch off. Fibroblasts stay activated, turn into contractile myofibroblasts and lay down too much collagen. Current treatments such as steroids, silicone and surgery mostly target how a scar looks, not that underlying process, and human models for testing new treatments are limited.

We built an in-vitro model by "licensing" human dermal fibroblasts with inflammatory cytokines so they behave like cells in a scarring wound. We then tested two cell-free treatments: PLAY, a growth-factor preparation developed by iCrest, and conditioned medium from umbilical cord mesenchymal stem cells (UC-MSC-CM).

My part was the IL-8 ELISA and the scratch migration assay, and I wrote the Results and Discussion for the paper. The work followed a six-week training program at iCrest, and I presented it to an audience including the Board of Directors at the annual iCrest Symposium.

## Methods

1. Cultured primary human dermal fibroblasts in DMEM at 37 °C and 5% CO₂.
2. Licensed the fibroblasts with TNF-α, IFN-γ and IL-1β to mimic the inflammatory surge that comes before scarring.
3. Set up six groups: untreated control, licensed only, licensed + 10% PLAY, licensed + 10% UC-MSC-CM, and each treatment alone on unlicensed cells to check it does not trigger inflammation by itself.
4. Measured COX-2 and α-SMA expression by semi-quantitative RT-PCR, normalized to GAPDH.
5. Measured secreted GM-CSF and IL-8 by sandwich ELISA.
6. Measured migration with a scratch-wound assay imaged at 0 and 48 hours, and contraction with a gel contraction assay.
7. Ran three independent replicates and compared groups with unpaired two-tailed t-tests.

## Technical challenges

- **Separating cause from contents.** UC-MSC-CM raised GM-CSF and IL-8, but the conditioned medium may already contain those cytokines. Without a medium-only blank, the increase cannot be pinned on the fibroblasts.
- **Short time window.** At 48 hours fibroblasts start to differentiate, but collagen deposition has not happened yet, so collagen I and III showed no change.
- **Migration is double-edged.** Faster wound closure is not always good. In licensed cells, more migration came with more α-SMA, a sign of pathological activation rather than healthy repair.

## Results and limitations

- Licensing raised COX-2, GM-CSF, IL-8, α-SMA and scratch closure compared with controls, so the model reproduced a pro-inflammatory, pro-fibrotic state.
- In licensed fibroblasts, PLAY lowered COX-2, GM-CSF and α-SMA (about two-fold for α-SMA) and reduced migration back toward control levels. Its reduction of IL-8 was not statistically significant.
- UC-MSC-CM lowered COX-2 and α-SMA, though not significantly, and raised GM-CSF and IL-8. On unlicensed cells it gave the greatest wound closure without raising COX-2 or α-SMA.
- Neither treatment triggered inflammation on its own. Overall, PLAY showed the most consistent anti-inflammatory and anti-fibrotic effect.

**Limitations.** A 2D monolayer of one cell type cannot reproduce layered skin. Migration was assessed qualitatively, several UC-MSC-CM trends need more replicates to reach significance, and the conditioned medium was not measured against a blank. Next steps include adding TGF-β1 to the licensing cocktail, longer time points for collagen, and a 3D or co-culture skin model.
