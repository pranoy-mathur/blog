**Purpose:** To count cells with a hemocytometer and calculate the percentage of live cells using trypan blue.

**Principle:**
A hemocytometer is a slide made for counting cells. It has a grid with four large corner quadrants (L1 to L4), each divided into 16 smaller squares. The central square is used for counting red blood cells. Each large quadrant holds a known volume of 0.1 µL (10^-4 mL), which is why the count is multiplied by 10^4. <!-- added: where the 10^4 factor comes from -->

Trypan blue is a differential stain that tells live and dead cells apart. A dead cell's membrane is damaged, so the dye passes through and stains the cell blue. A live cell's membrane keeps the dye out, so live cells look clear and bright. Crystal violet stains nuclei and is used to count white blood cells. <!-- my notes say crystal violet stains only live cells; it stains the nuclei of all cells -->

The calculations are:
- **Total cell count (TCC)** = average cells per quadrant x dilution factor x total volume (mL) x 10^4
- **Live cell count (LCC)** = average live cells per quadrant x dilution factor x total volume (mL) x 10^4
- **% viability** = LCC / TCC x 100

Cell counts are always written as a number times 10^6, in millions.

**Protocol:**
1. I mixed 10 µL of cell suspension with 10 µL of trypan blue, a 1:1 mix that gives a dilution factor of 2.
2. I loaded the mix onto the hemocytometer. <!-- added -->
3. Under the microscope, I counted the live (clear) and dead (blue) cells in each of the four corner quadrants, counting only the cells inside the 16 boxes.
4. I calculated the total cell count, live cell count and % viability with the formulas above.

**Results:**
I counted cells after trypsinization. My first count had a lot of variation between the quadrants, so I counted again:

| Quadrant | L1 | L2 | L3 | L4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Live cells | 105 | 100 | 93 | 91 |
| Dead cells | 0 | 2 | 1 | 1 |

This gave a total cell count of 5.89 x 10^6 cells.
