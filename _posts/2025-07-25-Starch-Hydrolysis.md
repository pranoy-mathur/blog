**Purpose:** To test whether bacteria from a sample and from the mouth can break down starch.

**Principle:**
Normal flora are the microorganisms that are always present on the skin and mucous membranes of every person without causing infection. Only bacteria and fungi count as normal flora. They stay in fairly stable conditions, either for a short or a specific period, and their composition varies from person to person. The mouth and gastrointestinal tract each have their own microbiota.

Saliva does several jobs: it starts food digestion, has antifungal, antibacterial and antiviral activity, coats and lubricates the mouth, acts as a buffer and helps mineralize teeth. Saliva contains the enzyme amylase, which breaks down carbohydrates.

Amylase hydrolyzes starch into maltose. <!-- from Lab Report doc --> When iodine is poured over a starch agar plate, it stains the remaining starch blue-black. If the bacteria produce amylase, the starch around their colonies has been broken down, so a clear zone appears around them. If they do not, the whole plate stains dark. <!-- added: wording of the iodine readout -->

To get individual colonies, I used streaking techniques such as quadrant, continuous and T-streaks. <!-- from Lab Report doc -->

**Protocol:**
<!-- protocol from Lab Report doc -->
1. I streaked the patient sample onto a starch agar (SA) plate.
2. Using a sterile swab, I swabbed my mouth before and after eating and streaked each swab onto a separate SA plate.
3. I incubated the plates for 24 hours at 37°C in the microbial incubator.
4. For the hydrolysis test, I covered each SA plate completely with iodine solution. <!-- doc says 10% iodine solution -->
