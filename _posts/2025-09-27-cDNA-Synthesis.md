**Purpose:** To make complementary DNA (cDNA) from isolated RNA using reverse transcriptase.

**Principle:**
The central dogma says that DNA is transcribed into RNA, which is translated into protein. Retroviruses can go backwards: they carry an enzyme called reverse transcriptase (RT), which copies their RNA into DNA so it can enter the host's genome. In the lab, RT is used to copy mRNA into cDNA. cDNA is different from genomic DNA because it only contains the genes that were being expressed. It is also much more stable than RNA, so it can be stored at -20°C or -80°C and used in PCR.

The reaction mix contains:
- **Reverse transcriptase**, the enzyme that builds the cDNA strand.
- **Oligo dT primers**, a string of T bases that binds to the poly-A tail (AAAAAA) at the 3' end of mRNA. This gives RT a starting point.
- **Random hexamers** (NNNNNN), short primers that bind at random places along the RNA.
- **Buffer**, which keeps the pH stable and supplies Mg2+ ions.
- **dNTPs**, the building blocks of the new strand. <!-- from Lab Report doc -->

The reaction runs at 37°C for 1 hour. Heating it to 100°C for 2 minutes then stops it by inactivating the enzyme. <!-- added: my notes on what the 100°C step does were unclear -->

**Materials:**
Master mix for one reaction:
- RT: 0.5 µL
- Oligo dT: 0.5 µL
- Random hexamers: 2 µL
- Buffer: 2 µL
- Total: 5 µL

**Protocol:**
1. I checked the RNA concentration and diluted the sample if it had more than 1000 ng in 5 µL.
2. I thawed, vortexed and briefly centrifuged the reagents, then prepared master mix for the number of reactions I needed.
3. In a PCR tube, I added 5 µL of master mix to 5 µL of RNA sample.
4. I incubated the tube at 37°C for 1 hour.
5. I heated it to 100°C for 2 minutes to stop the reaction.
6. I stored the cDNA at -20°C.
