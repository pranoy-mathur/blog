**Purpose:** To find how pH affects the precipitation of proteins from blood plasma.

**Principle:**
The charge on a protein depends on the pH around it. At its isoelectric point (pI), a protein has no net charge because it exists as a zwitterion. With no net charge, the protein molecules no longer repel each other, so they clump together and precipitate. That is why proteins are least soluble at their isoelectric point. <!-- added: why proteins precipitate at the pI -->

To keep proteins in solution, blood pH is held between 7.35 and 7.45. Carbonic acid (H2CO3) acts as a buffer: it can lose an H+ ion to form bicarbonate (HCO3-), or break down into water and carbon dioxide, which stops the blood becoming too acidic (acidosis) or too alkaline (alkalosis). <!-- from Lab Report doc -->

**Materials:**
<!-- from Lab Report doc -->
- Blood plasma
- Buffer solutions at pH 4, 7 and 10
- Pipettes and test tubes

**Protocol:**
<!-- protocol from Lab Report doc -->
1. I added each buffer to a separate test tube. <!-- the doc does not record the buffer volume -->
2. I added 500 µL of blood plasma to each test tube.
3. I incubated the tubes at room temperature and watched for precipitation.
