**Purpose:** To detach adherent cells from a culture dish using trypsin and EDTA so they can be counted and subcultured.

**Principle:**
Adherent cells stick to the dish using cell adhesion molecules (CAMs). Some CAMs depend on calcium (Ca2+), such as cadherins, integrins and selectins. Others do not, such as the immunoglobulin superfamily CAMs. <!-- added: corrected; my notes said immunoglobulins (IgGs) -->

Both the plastic of the dish and the cell membrane are negatively charged, so the dishes are coated with positively charged poly-L-lysine during manufacture. The CAMs bond to it, and the cells attach and spread.

Cells keep dividing until they cover the surface. When they touch, they stop dividing, which is called contact inhibition. If they stay too crowded, some cells start to die because they run out of space and nutrients. <!-- added: corrected; my notes described contact inhibition as cells signaling to kill each other --> So trypsinization is done at 70 to 80% confluency. A 35 mm dish holds about 1 million cells.

As an example, HeLa cells double about every 24 hours, so 0.2 x 10^6 seeded cells should reach about 1 x 10^6 in roughly 3.5 days. In practice it takes about 5 days because the cells first need time to adjust to the new dish.

Trypsin is a proteolytic enzyme, an endopeptidase that breaks peptide bonds between specific amino acids inside a protein chain. It cuts the bonds between the CAMs and the poly-L-lysine coating. EDTA is a chelator: it scavenges divalent ions such as Ca2+ and Mg2+, which loosens the calcium-dependent CAMs and makes trypsin's job easier. Before trypsin, cells have their normal shape; after, they round up and lift off.

Trypsin would eventually damage the cell membrane proteins and stress or burst the cells, so it is only left on for about a minute. Then DMEM with 10% FBS is added, because FBS contains α1-antitrypsin, which stops trypsin. Old (spent) medium is not reused because it contains dead cells and is low in nutrients. The saline wash removes leftover dead cells and medium. <!-- added: serum in leftover medium would block the trypsin -->

**Protocol:**
1. I checked the culture dish under the microscope for confluency, contamination and cell shape.
2. I removed and discarded the DMEM.
3. I added 1 mL of saline down the walls of the dish, swirled it gently to wash, then collected and discarded it.
4. I added 200 µL of trypsin-EDTA.
5. I incubated the dish for 1 minute.
6. I checked under the microscope that the cells had rounded up.
7. I added 1 mL of DMEM with 10% FBS.
8. I washed the dish with it and collected the cells in a 15 mL Tarsons tube.
9. I checked the dish again under the microscope to see if any cells were left behind.
10. I added 1 mL of saline, washed the dish again and collected it in the same tube.
11. I centrifuged the tube at 1500 rpm for 10 minutes.
12. I discarded the supernatant, added 1 mL of medium and counted the cells.

**Results:**
After trypsinization, my total cell count was 0.21 x 10^6 cells and my live cell count was 0.1075 x 10^6 cells.
