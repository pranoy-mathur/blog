**Purpose:** To immobilize invertase from yeast in calcium alginate beads.

**Principle:**
<!-- principle from Lab Report doc -->
Enzyme immobilization means confining enzyme molecules to a solid support so their movement is limited to a small region. Immobilization protects the enzyme from breaking down, lets it be reused for many reaction cycles, lets a reaction be stopped quickly by removing the enzyme, makes the enzyme more stable and keeps it out of the product.

The solid support is called a carrier. A good carrier is inert, physically strong, stable, cheap, reusable and reduces product inhibition. Carriers come in three types:
1. **Inorganic carriers**, such as porous glass, which handle high pressure but can wear down.
2. **Organic carriers**, such as cellulose derivatives, which are compatible with proteins.
3. **Organic synthetic carriers**, such as polystyrene, which have high chemical and mechanical stability.

Physical methods of immobilization include:
1. **Adsorption:** the enzyme sticks to the surface of a matrix such as silica or cellulose.
2. **Entrapment:** the enzyme is trapped inside a polymer matrix, such as a gel with pores that let the substrate in.
3. **Encapsulation:** a semi-permeable microcapsule forms around the enzyme so it cannot escape. It needs no chemical change to the enzyme and is easy to reuse, but some enzyme can leak through the pores.

In this experiment, I used entrapment in calcium alginate. When sodium alginate drips into calcium chloride, the calcium ions crosslink the alginate chains into gel beads, trapping the enzyme inside. <!-- added: crosslinking chemistry --> The enzyme was invertase from crude yeast extract. Invertase breaks sucrose down into glucose and fructose, which I later tested for in the Benedict's and DNSA experiments. <!-- added: invertase reaction -->

**Materials:**
- 0.1 M calcium chloride solution
- 1.5% sodium alginate solution
- Crude yeast protein extract
- Measuring cylinder, pipette and petri plate
- Distilled water

**Protocol:**
<!-- protocol from Lab Report doc -->
1. I prepared 50 mL of 0.1 M calcium chloride and kept it in the refrigerator to cool.
2. I prepared 50 mL of 1.5% sodium alginate.
3. I mixed 1 mL of crude yeast protein extract into 5 mL of sodium alginate.
4. I added this mixture dropwise to the calcium chloride in a petri plate.
5. I watched for beads forming.
6. I filtered the solution to collect only the beads.
