**Purpose:** To amplify the GAPDH gene from cDNA by PCR and check the product on an agarose gel.

**Principle:**
*PCR*

The polymerase chain reaction (PCR) makes millions of copies of one specific gene. Each cycle has three steps:
1. **Denaturation:** the temperature is raised so the two DNA strands separate.
2. **Annealing:** the temperature is lowered so the forward and reverse primers bind to either end of the target gene.
3. **Extension:** Taq DNA polymerase builds new strands from the primers using dNTPs.

Repeating this for about 32 cycles increases the amount of the gene enough to see it on a gel. <!-- from Lab Report doc --> I used JumpStart, a ready mix that contains Taq polymerase, dNTPs and buffer, so only the primers and template need to be added. <!-- added: what JumpStart contains --> The target was GAPDH, a housekeeping gene that is expressed at a steady level in most cells, so it is often used as a control. <!-- added: why GAPDH -->

*Agarose gel electrophoresis*

DNA is negatively charged, so in an electric field it moves toward the positive electrode. The agarose gel acts like a sieve: small fragments move faster and further than large ones. Ethidium bromide (EtBr) binds between the DNA bases and glows under UV light, which makes the bands visible. <!-- added: how the gel and EtBr work --> TAE (Tris-acetate-EDTA) buffer keeps the pH stable, and a DNA ladder of known sizes is run alongside the samples for comparison. If only the GAPDH gene was amplified, one clear band should appear. <!-- from Lab Report doc -->

**Materials:**
PCR master mix for one reaction:
- JumpStart: 6.5 µL
- Forward primer: 0.25 µL
- Reverse primer: 0.25 µL
- Total: 7 µL

**Protocol:**
*PCR*
1. I diluted the cDNA so that 1 µL contained 10 ng.
2. I prepared the master mix for the number of reactions I needed.
3. I added 7 µL of master mix and 5 µL of cDNA to each well of the PCR reaction plate.
4. I ran the plate in the thermal cycler. <!-- added: cycling conditions are not recorded in my notes -->

*Agarose gel*

5. I weighed 1 g of agarose to make a 2% gel in 50 mL of buffer.
6. I diluted 50x TAE buffer to 1x.
7. I prewashed the conical flask for 2 minutes to remove any leftover agarose.
8. I added the buffer to the agarose and boiled it at 100°C for 20 seconds, three times, then let it cool to 40 to 60°C.
9. I added 1.5 µL of ethidium bromide.
10. I poured it into the gel cast and let it set for 30 minutes.
11. I removed the comb and moved the gel to the buffer tank.
12. I loaded 10 µL of PCR product into the wells and 2 µL of DNA ladder into a separate well.
13. I ran the gel at 100 V for 15 minutes.
14. I viewed the gel under UV light. <!-- added -->
