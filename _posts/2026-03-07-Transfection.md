**Purpose:** To transfect HeLa cells with an EGFP plasmid using lipofection.

**Principle:**
<!-- principle and protocol from Lab Report doc -->
Transfection is a non-viral way of putting genes into mammalian cells. There are two types:
- **Transient transfection:** the DNA does not join the host genome. It is broken down by nucleases over time and is not passed on when the cells divide.
- **Stable transfection:** the DNA is integrated into the host genome, where the nucleus protects it, so it is passed down over many generations.

I used lipofection with Lipofectamine 3000. Lipofectamine is made of positively charged lipids that wrap around the negatively charged DNA to form a lipoplex. The lipoplex fuses with the cell membrane or is taken in by endocytosis, the DNA escapes into the cytoplasm and then reaches the nucleus. <!-- reworded; the doc's description of this step was unclear --> P3000 is an enhancer reagent mixed with the DNA. <!-- added -->

The plasmid codes for enhanced green fluorescent protein (EGFP), so cells that took up the plasmid glow green under a fluorescence microscope.

Another transfection reagent is polyethylenimine (PEI), which neutralizes the negative charge on the membrane so DNA can pass through. PEI is broken down inside the cell, releasing the DNA.

**Materials:**
- HeLa cells in a 24-well plate
- EGFP plasmid DNA
- Lipofectamine 3000 and P3000 reagent
- Culture media
- Rocker and incubator

**Protocol:**
<!-- the doc's steps repeated and contradicted each other; combined into one sequence following the standard Lipofectamine 3000 method -->
1. I cultured HeLa cells in a 24-well plate so that the cell number reached 0.16 x 10^6.
2. In tube A, I diluted 1 µL of Lipofectamine 3000 in 25 µL of media and vortexed for 2 to 3 seconds.
3. In tube B, I mixed 25 µL of media with 0.5 µg of plasmid DNA (3.4 µL of DNA stock) and 1 µL of P3000, then vortexed. <!-- assumed 3.4 µL is the volume containing 0.5 µg -->
4. I added the diluted DNA to the diluted Lipofectamine in a 1:1 ratio, mixed and incubated for 20 minutes.
5. I added 50 µL of the DNA-lipid mixture to the cells and incubated them on a rocker for 30 minutes.
6. I added 1 mL of media slowly down the side of the well and incubated the cells at 37°C for 24 to 48 hours. <!-- doc says 24-28 hours, likely a typo; 24-48 hours is the standard range -->
7. I checked the cells for green fluorescence under a fluorescence microscope.
