**Purpose:** To estimate the energy in different food samples by burning them and measuring the heat they release.

**Principle:**
<!-- added: the doc has no principle for this experiment; this section is standard textbook content -->
Food stores chemical energy. When a food sample burns, that energy is released as heat. If the burning sample heats a known mass of water, the energy the water absorbs can be calculated as:

Q = m x c x ΔT

where m is the mass of water (1 mL of water has a mass of about 1 g), c is the specific heat capacity of water (4.2 J/g°C) and ΔT is the rise in temperature. Dividing Q by the mass of food that actually burned gives the energy per gram. This method underestimates the true energy content because some heat escapes to the air and the tube.

**Materials:**
<!-- from Lab Report doc -->
- Food samples (such as peanut, rajma dal and chana dal)
- Weighing balance and spatula
- Boiling tube with distilled water
- Thermometer
- Lamp (or Bunsen burner) and aluminum foil

**Protocol:**
<!-- protocol from Lab Report doc -->
1. I weighed the initial mass of the food sample.
2. I measured 10 mL of distilled water into a boiling tube and noted its starting temperature.
3. I burned the food sample under the boiling tube and recorded the rise in temperature.
4. I weighed the food sample again after it had burned completely.
5. I used the formula to calculate the energy.
