**Purpose:** To separate proteins by molecular weight using sodium dodecyl sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE).

**Principle:**
Electrophoresis separates charged molecules in a mixture using an electric field. Charged molecules move through the field at a speed set by their charge to mass ratio. SDS-PAGE is used to separate protein mixtures and to estimate the molecular weight of each protein.

*Sample preparation*
- The protein sample (from RIPA lysis) is boiled for a few minutes with a buffer containing SDS and β-mercaptoethanol (BME).
- SDS is an anionic (negatively charged) detergent. It binds strongly to proteins, unfolds (denatures) them, and coats them with negative charge. Because every protein ends up negatively charged in proportion to its size, the proteins separate mainly by size. <!-- added: why SDS makes separation depend on size -->
- BME breaks the disulfide bridges in the protein.
- Glycerol makes the sample denser than the running buffer, so it sinks into the well. <!-- added: corrected; my notes said denser than the protein -->

*The gel*

The gel is made of polyacrylamide. Acrylamide monomers and N,N'-methylene bisacrylamide link into a crosslinked mesh. Ammonium persulfate (APS) provides the free radicals that start polymerization, and TEMED speeds up the release of those free radicals from APS. <!-- added: corrected; one line in my notes called TEMED an inhibitor --> The pore size depends on the acrylamide concentration. Gels can be made from 3% to 30%, and a higher percentage gives smaller pores.

Gels can be continuous (a single gel, or a gradient gel) or discontinuous. A discontinuous gel has two layers: a stacking gel on top (pH 6.8, larger pores) and a resolving (separating) gel below (pH 8.8, smaller pores).

*Running the gel*

The running buffer contains Tris-HCl and glycine. In the stacking gel at pH 6.8, glycine is mostly a zwitterion with almost no net charge, so it moves slowly. Chloride ions (Cl-) move fastest. The proteins get squeezed into a thin band between the fast Cl- ions and the slow glycine. This lines all the proteins up in one band before they enter the resolving gel.

In the resolving gel at pH 8.8, glycine becomes negatively charged and speeds up, overtaking the proteins (Cl- is still the fastest). The proteins, now in a narrow band, meet a higher pH, which tends to increase their mobility, and smaller pores, which decrease it. So the relative speed in the lower gel is Cl- > glycine > protein, and the proteins separate based on size and shape.

*Detection*

When the tracking dye reaches the bottom, the current is turned off. The gel is removed without pressure and placed in 7% acetic acid to keep the proteins from spreading. It is then shaken in Coomassie Brilliant Blue and moved into destain solution overnight, which removes the unbound dye. A ladder of known proteins runs alongside the samples so their sizes can be compared.

**Materials:**

| Component | 12% separating gel | 5% stacking gel |
|---|---|---|
| 30% acrylamide/bis-acrylamide | 2 mL | 0.4 mL |
| Distilled water | 1 mL | 1.7 mL |
| Tris-SDS buffer | 2 mL of 2.5x (pH 8.8) | 0.5 mL of 5x (pH 6.8) |
| 10% APS | 41.7 µL | 25 µL |
| TEMED | 6 µL | 3.3 µL |

**Protocol:**
1. I prepared the 12% separating gel, adding the APS and TEMED last. I poured the gel between the plates, immediately added distilled water on top to level it, and let it set for 30 minutes.
2. After 30 minutes, I poured off the water.
3. I prepared the 5% stacking gel. After adding the TEMED, I swirled gently to mix, poured it on top of the separating gel, immediately placed the comb (avoiding air bubbles) and let it set for 30 minutes.
4. I poured 1x Tris-glycine-SDS running buffer so that it connected the two electrodes, then carefully removed the comb.
5. I prepared each sample by mixing 20 µL of protein (from RIPA isolation) with 8 µL of sample loading dye and boiling it at 100°C for 3 minutes.
6. I loaded the samples in alternate wells: lane 1, prestained protein ladder; lane 2, prestained protein sample; lane 3, protein sample.
7. I connected the power supply and ran the gel at 120 V and 90 mA until the dye front reached 0.5 cm above the bottom of the gel.
8. I carefully removed the gel from between the plates with a spatula into a tray of distilled water, washed it for 1 minute and discarded the water.
9. I added 20 mL of water to the gel pieces with lanes 1 and 3 and went on to staining and destaining. For destaining, acetic acid helps the proteins keep the stain.
