**Purpose:** To isolate total RNA from cultured cells using Trizol.

**Principle:**
RNA is isolated for gene expression studies. Since mRNA is the copy of a gene that is being expressed, measuring it tells us which genes a cell is using and how much.

Trizol is a solution of phenol and guanidine isothiocyanate. It breaks open the cells and denatures proteins, including RNases, the enzymes that would otherwise degrade the RNA. <!-- added: composition and role of Trizol --> Adding chloroform and centrifuging splits the mixture into layers. RNA stays in the clear upper aqueous phase, while DNA and proteins go to the interphase and the lower organic phase. <!-- added: phase separation --> Isopropyl alcohol (IPA) makes the RNA come out of solution so it can be pelleted. The pellet is washed with 75% ethanol to remove salts, then dissolved in RNase-free water. <!-- added: role of IPA and ethanol -->

**Protocol:**
1. I added 0.5 mL of Trizol to 0.5 x 10^6 cells.
2. I added 150 µL of chloroform.
3. I incubated the tube on ice for 10 minutes.
4. I centrifuged it at 12,000 rpm for 15 minutes.
5. I collected the supernatant (aqueous phase) in a new 1.5 mL tube.
6. I added 500 µL of ice-cold IPA.
7. I incubated it on ice for 10 minutes.
8. I centrifuged it at 12,000 rpm for 10 minutes.
9. I discarded the supernatant and added 1 mL of ice-cold 75% ethanol.
10. I centrifuged it at 7,500 rpm for 7 minutes.
11. I discarded the supernatant, blotted the tube and let the pellet air dry.
12. I added 20 µL of RNase-free water to the RNA pellet.
