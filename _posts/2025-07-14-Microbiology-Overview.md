**Purpose:** To prepare different culture media, sterilize them, and use simple sterility tests to check for contamination in the lab.

**Principle:**
*Culture media*

Bacteria need nutrients to grow in the lab. A culture medium supplies macronutrients (glucose, proteins and lipids) and micronutrients (salts and minerals). Media can be grouped in three ways:
- **Physical nature:** solid (agar), liquid (broth) or semisolid.
- **Composition:** simple or basal media; complex media made with undefined ingredients such as beef extract and yeast extract; and chemically defined (synthetic) media.
- **Function:** selective media allow only certain bacteria to grow, and differential media make different bacteria look different.

The media I used were:
- **Nutrient agar:** a basic medium for general growth. I used it to compare sterile and non-sterile samples and for the glove point test.
- **LB (Luria-Bertani) agar:** an enriched medium for growing *E. coli* and fastidious bacteria.
- **EMB agar:** used to test for *E. coli*. *E. coli* ferments lactose, the acid produced lowers the pH, and the colonies turn a metallic green as a positive result. <!-- from Lab Report doc -->
- **Starch agar:** used to test if bacteria can break down starch.
- **Thioglycollate broth:** shows the oxygen requirement of bacteria. Oxygen is highest at the top of the tube and lowest at the bottom, so aerobes grow at the top, obligate anaerobes at the bottom, facultative anaerobes throughout, and microaerophiles a little below the surface. <!-- added: standard growth pattern; the doc leaves the microaerophile position blank -->
- **MacConkey broth:** selective for coliform bacteria such as *E. coli*.

*Sterilization*

Sterilization kills all microorganisms on a surface or in a material, while disinfection only reduces their number. <!-- from Lab Report doc --> The main methods are:
- **Heat:** dry heat in a hot air oven, or moist heat in an autoclave at 121°C and 15 psi. Moist heat kills microbes, including heat-resistant endospores, more effectively than dry heat. <!-- added: endospores; the doc's wording here was unclear -->
- **Radiation:** UV or gamma radiation.
- **Filtration:** filters such as HEPA filters, which trap particles down to about 0.2 to 0.3 µm.

*Sterility tests*

Sterility tests are quality control steps that help find where contamination is coming from:
- **Glove point test:** pressing gloved fingers onto agar shows whether the gloves are contaminated. Plates are checked at 24, 48 and 72 hours.
- **Sterile vs non-sterile swabs:** swabs from a sterile and a non-sterile surface are streaked on two halves of the same plate for comparison.
- **Water and saline testing:** samples are tested in MacConkey broth, where turbidity shows contamination. <!-- from Lab Report doc -->

**Materials:**
- Nutrient, LB, starch, thioglycollate, MacConkey and EMB agar powders
- Conical flasks, test tubes, cotton and paper
- Spatula, weighing scale and measuring cylinder
- Autoclave and laminar airflow (LAF) cabinet
- Swabs and 90 mm petri dishes

*Preparing the media*

| Medium | g per 1000 mL | Agar used (g) | Distilled water (mL) |
|---|---|---|---|

| LB agar | 25.00 | 6.500 | 260 |

1. I made cotton plugs for each conical flask and test tube.
2. I calculated the mass of each medium and the volume of distilled water needed, and added the powder first, then the water.
3. I fitted the cotton plugs and wrapped the flasks and tubes in paper.
4. I autoclaved the flasks and tubes.
5. I let the autoclaved flasks rest for 30 minutes.
6. I poured the agar into 90 mm petri dishes and let it solidify.

*Sterility tests*

7. I placed my gloved fingers on a nutrient agar plate for a few seconds and labeled it "glove point test."
8. I drew a line to split a second plate into two halves and labeled them "sterile" and "non-sterile."
9. Using a swab, I took samples from a sterile and a non-sterile surface and streaked them on the matching halves.
10. I incubated the plates for 24 to 48 hours.
