**Purpose:** To detect malaria antigen in a sample using an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA).

**Principle:**
An antigen is any substance the body sees as foreign and that can start an immune response, such as a pathogen, a virus, dust, pollen, or non-self cells and tissues. The body knows its own surface receptors, so when it detects a foreign one, an immune response is triggered. B cells release antibodies, which are proteins that bind to a specific antigen. The binding signals macrophages to engulf the antigen-antibody complex and break it down.

ELISA uses this antigen-antibody binding to detect a target molecule. One of the antibodies is linked to an enzyme. When a substrate is added, the enzyme turns it into a colored product, and a stop solution then changes the color again and ends the reaction. The plate is read in a spectrophotometer. More antigen means more bound enzyme, which means more color. The molecules stick to the plastic wells by adsorption, which means they are held on the surface by weak forces, not taken inside.

There are four main formats:
1. **Direct:** the antigen is in the well, and an enzyme-linked antibody binds to it directly. Unbound antibodies are washed off before the substrate is added.
2. **Indirect:** a primary antibody (anti-X) binds the antigen (X), and then an enzyme-linked secondary antibody (anti-anti-X) binds the primary one. Adding secondary and tertiary antibodies amplifies the signal.
3. **Sandwich:** a capture antibody coats the well, the antigen binds to it, and a detection antibody binds on top, so the antigen is "sandwiched." This format is used to detect cytokines.
4. **Competitive:** a labeled competitor that binds the same antibody is added along with the sample. If there is less antigen in the sample, more competitor binds, so a stronger signal means less antigen.

In the malaria kit, the enzyme on the conjugate is usually horseradish peroxidase (HRP), and the substrate is TMB, which turns blue and then yellow when the acidic stop solution is added. <!-- added: standard HRP/TMB chemistry used in malaria antigen kits --> That is why the plate is read at 450 nm.

**Protocol:**
1. I diluted the antibody reagent from 50x to 1x in sample diluent (20 µL of antibody reagent in 980 µL of diluent).
2. I added 25 µL of the positive control, negative control and sample (two replicates) to separate wells, and then added 100 µL of the diluted antibody reagent to each well.
3. I shook the plate to mix, applied a plate sealer and incubated it at 37°C for 30 minutes.
4. I washed the wells 6 times with a 30 second soak for each wash and blotted the plate dry.
5. I added 100 µL of diluted conjugate to each well and incubated the plate at 22 to 28°C for 30 minutes, then repeated the wash in step 4.
6. I added 100 µL of ready-to-use substrate and incubated the plate at 22 to 28°C for 30 minutes in the dark.
7. I added 100 µL of stop solution to each well to stop the reaction.
8. I read the plate at 450 nm within 30 minutes.
