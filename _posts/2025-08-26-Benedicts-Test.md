**Purpose:** To test for reducing sugars with Benedict's reagent and estimate their concentration in 1% sucrose, the products of immobilized invertase, and distilled water.

**Principle:**
Reducing sugars, such as glucose and fructose, can act as reducing agents. Benedict's reagent contains copper(II) ions, which react with the aldehyde group of a reducing sugar. The copper(II) ions are reduced to copper(I) oxide, which changes the color from blue to green, yellow, orange and finally brick-red as the sugar concentration increases. <!-- principle from Lab Report doc; color sequence added -->

Sucrose is not a reducing sugar, but invertase breaks it down into glucose and fructose, which are. <!-- added: why sucrose and the invertase products give different results --> So by comparing sucrose with the invertase products, the test shows whether the immobilized enzyme still works. Glucose standards of known concentration give a color scale to compare the samples against.

**Materials:**
- Glucose powder
- Benedict's reagent
- Distilled water
- Test tubes, pipettes and wells

**Protocol:**
<!-- protocol from Lab Report doc -->
1. I made 10 mL glucose solutions at 0.5%, 1%, 1.5% and 2%.
2. I added Benedict's reagent to each test tube.
3. I vortexed the tubes to mix them evenly.
4. I heated the tubes in a boiling water bath for a few minutes. <!-- added: Benedict's test needs heating; the doc does not list this step -->
5. I set up a tube with 10 mL of distilled water and tubes with each sample, labeled accordingly, and treated them the same way.
6. I compared the sample colors to the four glucose standards.
