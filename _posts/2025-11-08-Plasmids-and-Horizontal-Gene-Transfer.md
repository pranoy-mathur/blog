**Purpose:** To cover what plasmids are, how bacteria share genes, and the workflow I followed in the genetic engineering module.

**Principle:**
Plasmids are small, circular, double-stranded DNA molecules that replicate independently of the bacterial chromosome. They range from about 1 kilobase to hundreds of kilobases. Genes carried on plasmids give bacteria advantages such as antibiotic resistance, virulence factors and detoxification. Because they are small and easy to move between cells, plasmids are the main tool in genetic engineering, used in applications such as insulin production and cleaning up oil spills.

Bacteria can pass genes to each other without reproducing. This is called horizontal gene transfer, and it happens in three ways:
1. **Transformation:** a bacterium takes up foreign genetic material from its surroundings and expresses it.
2. **Conjugation:** DNA is transferred through a pilus from a donor cell to a recipient cell.
3. **Transduction:** bacterial DNA is carried from one bacterium to another by a virus.

Horizontal gene transfer is one reason antibiotic resistance spreads so quickly between bacteria. <!-- added -->

**Protocol:**
The module followed these steps, each covered in its own post:
1. Isolating plasmids from bacteria using the alkaline lysis method.
2. Inserting a plasmid into competent bacteria (transformation) and identifying the transformed bacteria with blue-white screening.
3. Introducing a plasmid into mammalian cells (transfection).
