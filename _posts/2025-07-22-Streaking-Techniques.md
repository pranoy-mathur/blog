**Purpose:** To isolate pure bacterial colonies from a mixed culture using streaking, pour plate and spread plate methods.

**Principle:**
In nature, microorganisms exist as mixed populations. To study one type, it has to be separated into a pure culture. Streaking has three objectives:
1. To obtain a pure culture from a mixed culture.
2. To propagate bacteria.
3. To obtain well-isolated colonies.

Streaking is based on dilution. As the inoculum is spread mechanically across the plate, it is gradually diluted until the bacterial cells are separated into individual cells, or colony forming units (CFUs). Each one grows into an isolated colony at the end of the streaks.

Types of streaking:
1. **Quadrant streak:** a smear is made in one section, and each following section is streaked from the end of the previous one. The last section has isolated colonies.
2. **Continuous streak:** a zigzag line runs from the smear across the whole plate.
3. **Radiant streak:** lines radiate out from the smear at one edge.
4. **T-streak:** the plate is divided into three sections by a T, and the inoculum is streaked from one section into the next.

Pure cultures can also be isolated by:
- **Pour plate method:** the inoculum is serially diluted until the colonies on the plate are separate.
- **Spread plate method:** the inoculum is spread evenly over the agar surface.

The number of bacteria in the original sample can be calculated as:

CFU/mL = (number of colonies x dilution factor) / volume plated (mL) <!-- added: corrected; my notes said "volume of culture plate" -->
