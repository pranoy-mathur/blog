**Purpose:** To isolate neutrophils from a blood sample and view them under a microscope after a phagocytosis assay with fluorescent beads.

**Principle:**
Neutrophils are granulocytes with a lobed nucleus. They make up 50 to 70% of white blood cells and are the first responders to bacterial and fungal infections, where they drive inflammation.

Neutrophils are separated from the other blood cells by density, using the same Ficoll-Paque density gradient as PBMC isolation. Ficoll-Paque has a density of 1.077 g/mL, which falls between the density of the mononuclear cells and the density of the neutrophils. After centrifugation, the mononuclear cells (density below 1.077 g/mL) collect as a buffy coat above the Ficoll layer. The red blood cells and the granulocytes, such as neutrophils and eosinophils (density above 1.077 g/mL), sink to the bottom of the tube. So for neutrophils, I kept the pellet instead of the buffy coat.

Most of the pellet is red blood cells, so they have to be removed. Ice-cold distilled water is hypotonic, so water moves into the red blood cells by osmosis until they swell and burst. <!-- added: standard explanation of hypotonic lysis --> Neutrophils survive a short exposure to water better than red blood cells do, so repeating the water wash and centrifugation leaves a pellet enriched in neutrophils. <!-- added: why neutrophils survive the lysis step -->

To see the neutrophils at work, I used a phagocytosis assay. Neutrophils are phagocytes, so they engulf the fluorescent beads added to the culture. Under a fluorescence microscope, the engulfed beads show up as bright points inside the cells. <!-- added: how the bead readout works -->

**Protocol:**
1. I diluted the blood with saline and layered it over Ficoll (1:3 ratio of Ficoll to blood) in a 15 mL tube without mixing the two layers, then centrifuged it at 1500 rpm for 20 minutes, the same as in the PBMC isolation.
2. I discarded the buffy coat (the PBMC ring).
3. I collected the pellet at the bottom of the tube.
4. I added 4 mL of ice-cold sterile distilled water to the red blood cell pellet and refrigerated it for 5 minutes, shaking the tube from time to time.
5. I centrifuged the tube for 10 minutes at 1500 rpm.
6. I discarded the supernatant.
7. I repeated steps 4 to 6 two more times.
8. I added complete medium (culture medium with serum) to the pellet and resuspended the cells. <!-- added: meaning of complete medium -->
9. I added 20 µL of diluted fluorescent beads to 2 mL of the cell suspension and cultured the cells overnight.
10. I observed the phagocytosed beads under a fluorescence microscope and took pictures.
