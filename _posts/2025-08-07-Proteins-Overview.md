**Purpose:** To cover the basics of protein structure and the main methods used to detect and measure proteins, and to run a Biuret test.

**Principle:**
Proteins are made of amino acids. Every amino acid has a central carbon attached to an amino group (NH2), a carboxyl group (COOH), a hydrogen and an R group. The R group is the part that varies between amino acids. Amino acids can be non-polar or polar, and polar ones can be positively or negatively charged. Polar amino acids are hydrophilic.

An amino acid can act as an acid or a base. At its isoelectric pH (pI), it exists as a zwitterion, which has both a positive and a negative charge but no net charge. Blood plasma is kept at a pH of about 7.35 to 7.45. <!-- added: plasma pH range from the Lab Report doc; the notebook line is cut off -->

Proteins have four levels of structure: primary, secondary, tertiary and quaternary. They can also be grouped by role into structural proteins and dynamic proteins, such as enzymes.

Protein assays can be qualitative (is protein present?) or quantitative (how much?). The main ways to measure protein are:
- **UV spectrophotometry:** proteins absorb UV light at 280 nm because of their aromatic amino acids, which have ring structures. Nucleic acids absorb at 260 nm.
- **Colorimetric assays:** the Bradford assay, where Coomassie Brilliant Blue (CBB) dye changes color; the Lowry method; the BCA method; and the Biuret test.
- **Fluorescent assays.**

In the Biuret test, Cu2+ ions in an alkaline solution form a complex with the nitrogen atoms of peptide bonds, which changes the color from blue to purple. The Lowry and BCA methods go a step further: the Cu2+ is reduced to Cu+, which then reacts with a second reagent, making them more sensitive. <!-- corrected: my notes put the Cu2+ to Cu+ reduction under Biuret; it is the basis of Lowry and BCA --> Because it detects peptide bonds, the test is not specific to any one protein, and it is less sensitive than the Bradford assay.

Protein quantification and purification are used in areas such as food inspection and clinical testing.

2. For the blank, I used 50 µL of distilled water with the Biuret reagent.
3. I transferred 200 µL of each mixture into wells of a plate, in duplicate.
