**Purpose:** To detect senescent cells by staining for senescence-associated β-galactosidase.

**Principle:**
Normal cells can only divide a limited number of times. After about 40 to 60 divisions, they stop dividing permanently but stay alive. This is called senescence. <!-- my notes say 40-42; the usual range is about 40 to 60 -->

Senescence can be measured semi-quantitatively in three ways: by histochemical staining, by flow cytometry, or by measuring gene expression of markers such as p16.

This assay looks for β-galactosidase, which builds up in cells as they become senescent. The staining mixture contains X-gal. β-galactosidase cuts X-gal into a blue product, so senescent cells turn blue. The senescence-associated form of the enzyme is detected at pH 6.0, so the staining is pH dependent. <!-- added: pH 6.0 --> The plate is kept in an incubator without CO2, because CO2 would change the pH of the stain. <!-- added: why no CO2 -->

**Materials:**
Staining mixture:

| Component | For 10 mL | For 1.5 mL |
|---|---|---|
| Staining solution | 1 mL | 0.15 mL |
| Reagent A | 125 µL | 18.75 µL |
| Reagent B | 125 µL | 18.75 µL |
| X-gal | 0.25 mL | 0.0375 mL |
| Ultra-pure water | 8.5 mL | 1.275 mL |

I also made 1x PBS (200 µL of 10x PBS with 1800 µL of water) and 1x fixation buffer (200 µL of 10x fixation buffer with 1800 µL of water).

**Protocol:**
1. I aspirated the growth medium from the cells.
2. I washed the cells twice with 1 mL of 1x PBS per well, carefully removing all the wash solution so the cells did not detach.
3. I added 1.5 mL of 1x fixation buffer per well and incubated the plate for 6 to 7 minutes at room temperature, as the kit instructs.
4. While the cells were fixing, I prepared the staining mixture.
5. I rinsed the cells 3 times with 1 mL of 1x PBS per well.
6. I added 1 mL of staining mixture per well.
7. I sealed the plate with Parafilm so it would not dry out and incubated it at 37°C without CO2 until the cells were stained blue (2 hours to overnight). The exact time has to be optimized.
8. I observed the cells under a microscope, counted the blue-stained cells and the total number of cells, and calculated the percentage of cells expressing β-galactosidase (senescent cells).
9. After staining, if needed, the staining mixture can be replaced with 1x PBS.
10. For long-term storage, the staining mixture can be removed and the cells covered with 70% glycerol and stored at 2 to 8°C.
