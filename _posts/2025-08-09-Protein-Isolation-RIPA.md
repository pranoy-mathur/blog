**Purpose:** To isolate total protein from mammalian cells using RIPA lysis buffer.

**Principle:**
Protein isolation lets us identify, purify and analyze gene expression at the protein level. In cell lysis, a detergent reacts with the cell membrane and breaks it down, so the contents of the cell are released. This method collects total protein, not one specific protein. Membrane proteins can only be dissolved (solubilized) with a detergent. Detergents are amphipathic, which means they have both polar and non-polar parts.

A lysis buffer system usually contains:
1. **A pH buffer**, such as Tris-HCl, which also keeps the osmotic balance.
2. **Salt ions**, which keep proteins stable by increasing the ionic strength of the medium. If the salt concentration is too high, some proteins may precipitate.
3. **Chaotropic agents**, which weaken the hydrophobic interactions in proteins to solubilize them. They unravel hydrophobic regions by disrupting hydrogen bonds.
4. **Ionic detergents**, such as SDS (sodium dodecyl sulfate).
5. **Protease inhibitors.** Lysis also releases proteases, which would digest the target proteins, so inhibitors are added to stop them. <!-- added: corrected; my notes said inhibitors are released during lysis -->
6. **Reductants**, such as DTT or BME, which break the bonds holding the protein structure together and denature it.

RIPA (radioimmunoprecipitation assay) buffer contains:
- 150 mM NaCl
- 1.0% NP-40 or Triton X-100
- 0.5% sodium deoxycholate
- 0.1% SDS
- 50 mM Tris, pH 8.0

The detergents can later be removed by column chromatography if needed. Proteins have their maximum absorbance at 280 nm, while DNA and RNA absorb most at 260 nm, so the isolated protein can be checked and measured.

**Protocol:**
1. I centrifuged the cells at 1500 rpm for 10 minutes to get a pellet.
2. I added 200 µL of RIPA buffer to 1 x 10^6 cells.
3. I dissolved the pellet in the buffer by vortexing.
4. I added 2 µL of protease inhibitor (a 1:100 dilution) and mixed it. <!-- notes unclear between 200 µL and 2 µL; 1:100 is the standard dilution -->
5. I incubated the tube in an ice bath for 15 minutes.
6. I centrifuged it at 12,000 rpm for 10 minutes.
7. I collected the supernatant in a fresh tube.
8. I stored it at -20°C for later use and quantification.
