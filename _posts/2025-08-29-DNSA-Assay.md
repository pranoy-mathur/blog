**Purpose:** To measure the concentration of reducing sugars in three samples using the DNSA method.

**Principle:**
DNSA reagent contains 3,5-dinitrosalicylic acid. When heated with a reducing sugar, it oxidizes the sugar's aldehyde or ketone group and is itself reduced to 3-amino-5-nitrosalicylic acid (ANSA). This turns the solution from yellow to orange-red. <!-- color change added --> The intensity of the color depends on how much reducing sugar is present, and it is measured in a spectrophotometer. Using a standard graph of known sugar concentrations, the absorbance of each sample can be converted into a concentration.

Unlike Benedict's test, which gives a color I compared by eye, the DNSA method gives a number, so it is quantitative.

**Materials:**
- DNSA reagent
- Sucrose powder
- Distilled water
- Test tubes, pipettes and wells
- Spectrophotometer

**Protocol:**
<!-- protocol from Lab Report doc -->
1. I added 200 µL of each sample to separate test tubes.
2. I added 1 mL of DNSA reagent to each tube and mixed well with the pipette.
3. I heated the tubes in a boiling water bath for a few minutes and let them cool. <!-- added: the DNSA reaction needs heat; the doc does not list this step -->
4. I transferred 300 µL of each solution to separate wells.
5. I read the absorbance at 545 nm. <!-- doc says 545 nm; 540 nm is the usual wavelength -->
6. I calculated the concentration of reducing sugars using the standard graph.
