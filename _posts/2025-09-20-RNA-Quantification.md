**Purpose:** To measure the purity and concentration of isolated RNA using UV absorbance.

**Principle:**
Nucleic acids absorb the most UV light at 260 nm, and proteins absorb the most at 280 nm. Measuring both tells us how much RNA there is and how clean it is.

- **Purity** is the ratio A260/A280. Pure DNA gives about 1.8, and pure RNA gives about 2.0. A lower ratio means protein contamination.
- **Concentration (ng/µL)** = A260 x dilution factor x 40. The factor of 40 is used because an A260 of 1 equals about 40 µg/mL of RNA. <!-- added: meaning of the 40 factor -->

The next step, cDNA synthesis, needs no more than 1000 ng of RNA in 5 µL. If the sample is more concentrated, it is diluted with nuclease-free water. For PCR later on, 1 µL of cDNA should contain 10 ng.

**Protocol:**
1. I washed the cuvette with distilled water.
2. I filled the blank cuvette with 3 mL of distilled water and blanked the readings at 260 nm and 280 nm.
3. I thawed and vortexed the RNA sample.
4. In the test cuvette with 3 mL of water, I removed 5 µL of water and added 5 µL of the RNA sample, giving a 600-fold dilution.
5. I measured the absorbance at 260 nm and 280 nm.
6. I calculated the purity and concentration, and diluted the RNA with nuclease-free water if it was above 1000 ng per 5 µL.
