**Purpose:** To isolate genomic DNA from cells using SDS lysis and phenol-chloroform-isoamyl alcohol (PCI) extraction.

**Principle:**
To get DNA out of a cell, the cell has to be broken open, and everything that is not DNA has to be removed. Each reagent in this method does one part of that job.

- **SDS (sodium dodecyl sulfate)** is a detergent. It breaks down the cell membrane so the contents are released, a step called lysis. It also disrupts protein-protein interactions.
- **TE buffer (Tris-EDTA)**: Tris keeps the pH stable. EDTA chelates divalent metal ions such as Mg2+ and Ca2+, which nucleases need to work. Without these ions, the nucleases cannot degrade the DNA.
- **Proteinase K** digests proteins that would otherwise contaminate the DNA, which gives higher purity.
- **RNase** breaks down RNA, so only DNA is left.
- **PCI (phenol:chloroform:isoamyl alcohol)**: phenol denatures the proteins. Chloroform helps the mixture separate into layers: an organic phase at the bottom, an interphase, and an aqueous phase on top. Proteins and lipids go into the organic phase, while the nucleic acids stay in the aqueous phase. Isoamyl alcohol reduces foaming. <!-- added: role of isoamyl alcohol -->
- **Cold ethanol** makes the DNA come out of solution so it can be pelleted, because DNA is not soluble in ethanol. <!-- added: why ethanol precipitates DNA -->

**Protocol:**
1. I centrifuged the cells at 1500 rpm for 10 minutes.
2. I discarded the supernatant and added 250 µL of 1% SDS and 250 µL of TE buffer to the pellet.
3. I vortexed the tube.
4. I added 3 µL of proteinase K and 3 µL of RNase.
5. I added 700 µL of PCI, vortexed it quickly and incubated it on ice for 10 minutes.
6. I centrifuged the tube at 8000 rpm for 15 minutes.
7. I collected the aqueous (top) phase in another tube.
8. I added cold ethanol at a 1:2 ratio of aqueous layer to ethanol.
9. I centrifuged the tube at 5000 rpm for 10 minutes.
10. I air dried the pellet and added 20 µL of RNase-free water.
