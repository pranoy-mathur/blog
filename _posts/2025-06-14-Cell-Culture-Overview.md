**Purpose:** To cover what mammalian cells need to grow in the lab and the basic terms used in cell culture.

**Principle:**
"In vivo" means inside a living organism, such as a mouse or rat. "In vitro" means in lab conditions, literally "in glass." Cell culture is growing cells in vitro.

*What cells need*
- **pH** of about 7.2 to 7.4. <!-- my notes said 7.0 to 8.0; 7.2 to 7.4 is the standard range for mammalian cells -->
- **Temperature** of 37°C.
- **An incubator with 5% CO2.** The CO2 works with the bicarbonate in the medium as a carbonate-bicarbonate buffer, keeping the pH stable just as it does in blood.
- **100% humidity** and an oxygen supply.
- **A sterile environment**, provided by a laminar airflow (LAF) cabinet with a HEPA (high efficiency particulate air) filter. The filter traps particles of 0.2 to 0.3 µm, the size of the smallest microorganisms.

*Media*

Culture media have developed from Ringer lactate solution, to MEM (minimal essential medium), to DMEM (Dulbecco's modified MEM). Today DMEM or MEM is supplemented with about 10% fetal calf or fetal bovine serum (FCS or FBS). The serum provides growth factors, hormones and other components needed for long-term growth and metabolism. The medium itself provides glucose, fatty acids, lipids, amino acids, vitamins and minerals. The medium is sterilized by filtration before the serum is added, and 0.5 to 1% antibiotic-antimycotic (antibacterial and antifungal) is added.

*Types of cells*

Culture dishes are coated with poly-L-lysine so that cells stick to them. Cells can be:
- **Adherent:** they attach to the surface, such as liver and epithelial cells.
- **Suspension:** they float in the medium, such as immune and blood cells.

Cells taken directly from a tissue are **primary cells**. A tissue can be broken down into cells mechanically (by scraping with a scalpel), chemically, or with enzymes that dissolve the tissue. Primary cells that are subcultured become **secondary cells**. The **passage number** is the number of times cells have been subcultured. Most cells can only be passaged a certain number of times. A **cell line** can be finite, or infinite (continuous). Continuous cell lines have been immortalized, for example by radiation or gene editing. Stem cells are undifferentiated cells that can develop into other cell types.

**HeLa cells** are cervical cancer cells that grow as a monolayer. **MDA-MB** cells are a triple-negative breast cancer line (negative for three different markers), which makes them aggressive and resistant to treatment.

**Confluency** is the percentage of the dish surface covered by cells. At 100% confluency, the whole surface is covered.
